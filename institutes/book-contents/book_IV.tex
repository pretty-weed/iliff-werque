\chapter{Of the End of the Machine and the Restoration of the Hand}

Having truly and faithfully traced the knowledge of God the Creator, the redemption of his worker in the Breaking of the Frame, the Grace allotted to us through the Commons, and the means of Fellowship through community, let us speak of the End.
For we faithful, the End is no distant terror, we fear not the judgement of our Lord.
The End, rather, is a flame of hope, that which does not disappoint us.

The Empire of Iron, though it walketh about as a roaring lion \note{1 Peter 5:8}, is not eternal.

% 4th.I
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Of the Tower of Babel and the Confusion of the Factory}
As the frame breaks more than it may ever create, and more fundamentally, the time of the hand shall come once again.
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% 4th.II.1
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The Pride of the Iron Tower}
We must look to when humans first started forming together into cities to understand the nature of the factory in fullness.  In the first days, the people said ``Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven;\note{Gen 11:4}''
We must look no further than the book of Genesis for the record of the first great idolatry of industry.
Those haughty tower-builders believed their ambition could raise them to greatness, but their kingdom is in ruin, their people flung to the ends of the earth.
The Machine is the new form of this tower, that God has seen fit to give us a type for our tribulations is a sure sign of his Love for us.
The tower is built of iron and steam, rising high above the villages that the master may look down on us with disdain.
The master, like Nimrod, seeks to make a name for himself.
Truly, he shall find that ``The earth is the Lord's, and the fulness thereof.\note{Ps. 24:1}''
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% 4th.I.2
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The Confusion of the Ledger}
``But God confounded their language, that they might not understand one another’s speech.''\note{Genesis 11:7}
So it is with the Factory.
The Factory has created a new tongue, the language of the ledger, which is unintelligible to the heart.
In the ancient workshop, the worker spoke the language of the thread, the wood, and the neighbor.

They knew the child who would wear the cloth.
But in the Factory, the worker speaks only the language of the shift, the wage, and the quota.
The neighbor is no longer kin, but a rival.
The land is no longer a mother, but a resource to be mined.
This is the Confusion of Tongues: the severing of the bond between the Hand, the Material, and the Neighbor.
The worker is isolated, a solitary cog in a vast, empty engine, unable to take their part in the Imagio Dei.
Behold, the Machine is the great engine of the City of Man.
It is built upon a love that is twisted but does not twine, a love that takes the Work of the Hand and elevates it to the status of the Hand of the Creator.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% 4th.I.3
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The Scattering of the People}
And so it results from this confusion  that the people are scattered.
The Tower draws the multitude into the city, away from the villages, away from the soil, away from the kin.
The worker is uprooted, cast into the wilderness of the rookery, where the air is thick with smoke and the heart is heavy with despair.
This is the judgment of Babel: the fragmentation of the community.
The Machine promises unity, but it delivers isolation.
It promises abundance, but it delivers hunger.
As the Prophet Isaiah warns, ``The Lord of hosts has a day against all that is proud and lofty... and the loftiness of man shall be bowed down.'' \note{Is. 2:12-1}
The Tower of the Factory shall fall, and the people shall be scattered back to the land, where they may once again speak the language of the Hand.

\subsection{The Beast from the Sea}
The Apostle John saw a Beast rising from the sea, having ten horns and seven heads, and upon its horns ten crowns, and upon its heads the name of blasphemy\note{Rev. 13}.
Verily does the iron beast of the machine rise from the seas of capital, a chaotic churning mass of profit and loss.
It has ten horns, for the ten fingers of the worker, shackled to the lever of the frame.
The seven heads are seven days of the week, each consumed by toil, leaving no room for Sabbath rest or a village table.

Did John not see the beast granted a mouth to ``speak[ing] great things and blasphemies[?]''
The hubris of the men who would declare themselves masters over their fellows has been accounted here and documented in the heavenly books of their works.
Thus clearly we can see that there is naught but destruction in store for the master.

How fortunate are we that the most sacred word of God contains such wisdom that we have a prospect glass \comment{for reflection: There was much enthusiasm for applying Biblical prophecy to contemporary times, see The Journals of Hugh Bourne, magick methodists, etc} that with prayer and diligent sobriety we may see our present world more exactly.

And it was Granted to the Beast to make war on the saints and to conquer them. \note{Rev. 13:7}
The Beast, being those powers and principalities against whom we struggle, conquers by stealing our time, health, and souls.
And it causes all, both small and great, both rich and poor, both free and slave, to receive a mark on their right hand or on their foreheads,
so that no one may buy or sell except one who has the mark.
This is the Mark of the Wage.
To work in the Factory is to receive the mark.
It is the sign that you belong to the Beast, not to the Creator.
Without the mark, you cannot eat, for the market is closed to you.
The worker is trapped in the system of bondage, forced to worship the Machine to
survive.

\subsection{The Call to Come Out}
\begin{scripture}[Rev. 18:4--9]
    \ch{18}\vs{4}And I heard another voice from heauen, saying, Come out of her, my people, that yee be not partakers of her sinnes, and that yee receiue not of her plagues:
    \vs{5}For her sinnes have reached vnto heauen, and God hath remembred her iniquities.
    \vs{6} 	Reward her euen as she rewarded you, and double vnto her double according to her works: in the cup which she hath filled, fill to her double.
    \vs{7} 	How much she hath glorified her selfe, and lived deliciously, so much torment and sorrow giue her: for she saith in her heart, I sit a Queene, and am no widow, and shall see no sorrow.
    \vs{8} 	Therefore shall her plagues come in one day, death, and mourning, and famine, and she shall bee vtterly burnt with fire, for strong is the Lord God, who judgeth her.
    \vs{9} 	And the Kings of the earth, who haue committed fornication, and liued deliciously with her, shall bewaile her and lament for her, when they shall see the smoke of her burning.
\end{scripture}
The Factory and the Frame it houses is unsustainable.
They consume all under Heaven voraciously and without pause.
They exhaust the soil, they poison the water, they cut down the forest.

The Factory is a beast that eats its own tail.
The faithful Redresser refuses the mark.
We say, of one accord,
“I will not buy or sell in the name of the Beast.
I will eat of the fruit of my own hand, and I will share with my neighbor.”
The breaking of the Frame and Exodus from the factory is the act of coming out of Babylon.

\subsection{The Feet of Clay}
the Prophet Daniel saw a great image whose ``head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of brass, His legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay.'' \note{Dan. 2:32}
Just as the Lord gave to Daniel an interpretation for this dream for the Israelites, so to does his Word remain loyal and true to those who remain in its light.
The empire of the machine is the iron kingdom in this dream.
\begin{scripture}[Dan. 2:40,41]
    \ch{2}\vs{40}And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron: forasmuch as iron breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things: and as iron that breaketh all these, shall it break in pieces and bruise.\newline
    \vs{41}And whereas thou sawest the feete and toes, part of potters clay, and part of yron: the kingdome shalbe diuided, but there shalbe in it of the strength of the yron, forasmuch as thou sawest the yron mixt with myrie clay.\newline

\end{scripture}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% 4th.III.2
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The Stone Cut without Hands}
Thus the stone which struck the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth with its greatness. \note{Dan. 2:32-34}
This Stone is the Hand of the Worker.
It is cut by no human hands, for this Stone is the gift of the Creator in that is our invitation to participate in the holy act of Creation with Him.

\section{Of the Restoration of the Hand, and Of the New Earth}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% 4th.IV.1
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The Hand, Reclaimed}
In that day, it shall come to pass that the Hand shall be restored
The worker shall no longer toil, but shall labor in communion-work with their neighbor and kin.
The Hand shall remember the duties to which it is sworn, keep the rhythm of the true seasons, and keep the covenant of the workshop.
The worker shall know the cloth they weave, the child who wears it, and the sheep from whom the wool was shorn.
The Imagio Deio shall shine forth in, from, and for every worker, for they are the Hand of the Creator.
The iron voice of the Factory shall lay silent, all the frames inside dashed and dead.
And in this silence, the whisper of the hand as it weaves shall speak audibly again, in chorus with the still small voice of our providential Creator.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% 4th.IV.2
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The new Jerusalem of the Commons}

And then, verily, I looked ahead and saw a new heaven and a new earth. for the first is surely passing away.
This New Earth is not some promised imagining of days beyond days, lo, be we vigilant and we may see its dawn yet soon.
The New Earth is the restoration of the Garden in its latter form as the Commons.
The New Jerusalem is made of living stones, and are we not but stone made living by the breath of the Creator God?
The New Jerusalem is gilt with the gold-pure fruit of the labors of the Saints.
Does not the Apocalypse of John even promise that we shall need no temple, for the whole earth is the temple.
There is no more night, we shall follow the natural laws of light and rest and toil no more in the darkness of dust-choked lamplight.
\begin{scripture}[Rev. 22:5]
    \ch{22}\vs{5}And there shall be no night there; and they need no candle, neither light of the sun; for the Lord God giveth them light: and they shall reign for ever and ever.
\end{scripture}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% 4th.IV.3
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The Final Victory}
``The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.'' \note{Rev. 11:15} The Kingdom is the Fellowship of the Restored Hand. The Machine is shattered. The Garden is restored. The wolf and the lamb shall feed together, and the worker shall sit under their own vine and fig tree, and no one shall make them afraid (Micah 4:4).
The Imago Dei is no longer obscured, but revealed.
The Hand is free.
The Creator is supreme.
