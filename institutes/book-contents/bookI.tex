\subsection{On the Factory as the Disordered Frame of Disordered Loves}
\pstart It benefits us much to begin here with the wisdom of the Blessed Augustine who taught us that all sin is but a disordering of loves.
``Accordingly, two cities have been formed by two loves: the earthly by the love of self, even to the contempt of God; the heavenly by the love of God, even to the contempt of self.''\ledsidenote{Aug. City of God Lib. 14 Cap. 28} \pend % Todo cite this more correctly

\pstart The Industrialist, in his pride, has inverted this order.
He looks at the Frame, the Loom, the Steam Engine, and he says, ``This is my god.''
\sidenote{Exod. 15:2}
He loves the creation more than the Creator.
He loves hoarding his profit more than his neighbor.
He loves efficiency more than the Sabbath rest.
This is the Disorder of the Machine.
It is not that the tool is evil in itself;
the hammer is good, when it is exercised towards building the Kingdom of God.
The plow is good, when the fruits of our fields are shared equally.
But when the hammer is loved as an end in itself, when the plow is worshiped as the source of life, it becomes an Idol. \pend

\pstart As Augustine warned, ``To love the creature more than the Creator is to make the creature a god, and to make oneself a slave.
''\sidenote{Aug. Confess. lib. 10, cap. 29}
The worker in the Factory is a slave to this disordered love.
They are forced to love the rhythm of the wheel, to love the speed of the belt, to love the ledger of the master.
% I've got half a mind to work in something from the futurist manifesto about the speed of the bullet being beautifuls
Their heart is restless, for they are trying to find rest in the Machine, but the Machine cannot rest.
It must always turn, always produce, always consume. \pend

\pstart Therefore, the Silent Strike is not an act of hate, but an act of Love.
When we Strike in Silence, we conduct of re-ordering the heart.
It is the declaration: ``I will not love the Frame more than the Creator. I will not love the Profit more than the Neighbor. I will use the tool, but I will not worship it. I will not give unto Caesar that which is God's.'' \pend

\pstart The breaking of the Frame is the breaking of the idol, the restoration of the Order of Love, and the return to the City of God, where the Hand is free and the Creator is supreme. \pend

\subsection{The Distinction between the Maker and the Made}
\pstart Thus, the master commits the most fundamental and critical error of our age: the confusion of the Maker with the Made.
The master, in his hubris, commits the same error and sin as the idolater when he takes the wood of the forest, and the iron of the earth, forged by sweat of brow, has from them constructed a frame, and imagines this frame to provide abundance\sidenote{Isaiah 44:9-20}.
In the Creator's image, we were made artisans. \pend

\pstart He is the ultimate artisan, he formed the world into being and saw that it was good.
He gave the human the Hand to be a co-creator, to shape clay and weave the thread in communion with the Divine.
This is the Order of Love\sidenote{Aug. City of God Lib 15 Cap. 22}: to love and enjoy the Creator first, and to not love the created more than the creator. \pend

\subsection{On the Hand as the Image of God}

\pstart Before there was a Factory, there was a Hearth.
Before there was enclosure, there were commons.
Before there was a Frame, there was the Hand. \pend

\pstart The Imago Dei is not a fixed stamp upon the soul, but an active and living capacity of the Hand to bring into being in communion with the Creator.
It is by His Grace and beneficence that we have been granted this. \pend

\pstart In the ancient workshop, we had through the blessing of our labors the knowledge of everything of import that was about us.
The worker knew the cloth they wove, and the cloth they wore, as it was of one cloth.
The worker clothed their neighbor, and knew that their neighbor's child was warm.
The worker ate of their neighbor's field, and knew the earth and plants of the earth that gave them sustenance.
\pend

\subsection{On the violations of covenant demanded by the Machine}
\pstart
The Machine demands constant production, violating the Sabbath rhythm of rest.
The Factory isolates the worker, turning our labors into toils, and keeping us from fellowship and communion.
Just as when the threads are pulled apart the cloth unravels, when the master isolates the workers, he pulls the weft of the people from the warp of their community.
\pend

\subsection{On the Ghastly Hand and how it turns neighbor against neighbor}
\pstart
The Market treats neighbors as competitors rather than kin, breaking the covenant of community.
Before we were set upon each other in the marketplace at the behest of the prophet of Mamon Dr. A. Smith, we were bound together at the hearth.
\pend
\pstart
Now, the ledger is our only law, and we have accepted the mark of the wage.
The so-called ``invisible hand'' of the market is a demonic parody of Divine Providence.
When it promises to grant life, instead it leaves us with death.
How false its boasts of industry, it unmakes more than it can make.
\pend
